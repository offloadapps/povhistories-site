EP003 · An Ordinary Day · c. 1150 BCE · Deir el-Medina, Thebes, New Kingdom Egypt

Paid in Grain

In the village of the royal tomb builders at Thebes, it is your turn to stay home and take in the deliveries: firewood, water and the month's pay in grain. Then you write it down on a flake of limestone.

What the film simplifies

The workman is a composite. The duty of staying home to receive deliveries rotated through the crew; the film puts the firewood, water and the month's grain on the same morning, which compresses deliveries that came on their own schedules. Wage figures varied with rank and period; "about four sacks of emmer and one and a half of barley" is the commonly cited workman's ration. The kitchen layout and grinding stone follow the village's excavated houses in general, not one house.
